Privacy Policy — Burn Locker

Effective date: September 29, 2026

This Privacy Policy explains what data Burn Locker (the “Service”) handles, where it lives, and — just as importantly — what we never see. Burn Locker is built so that this policy can be short: there is no account, no server, and no sync, so there is very little to describe.

1. The short version

We do not collect your data. Burn Locker runs entirely on your own device (in your browser, as an extension, or as a mobile web app). Your master password, your saved logins, and anything else you enter is encrypted and decrypted locally, using your master password as the key. Nothing is transmitted to us, because there is no server for it to be transmitted to.

2. What data exists, and where

  • Your saved vault entries (titles, usernames, passwords, notes) are encrypted with a key derived from your master password and stored only in your browser’s local storage on your own device (chrome.storage.local for the extension, the browser’s local storage for the web app). We have no access to this storage, encrypted or otherwise.
  • Your master password is never stored in any form — not encrypted, not hashed to disk, nowhere. It is held in memory only while you’re actively using an unlocked vault, and is discarded when you lock the vault, close the browser, or after 15 minutes of inactivity.
  • A temporary session key, derived from your master password, is cached in memory (chrome.storage.session on the extension) so you’re not asked to unlock repeatedly during one browser session. This is cleared automatically when you lock the vault or close the browser — it never touches disk.
  • Your theme preference (light/dark mode) is stored separately from your vault, so it survives even if you delete your vault.
  • A list of hostnames you’ve told the “save this login?” banner never to show for (see Section 4) is stored locally, separately from your vault entries.
  • Exported files. If you use Export to move entries to another device, the resulting file is encrypted with your master password and saved wherever your browser saves downloads. We never see this file. You are responsible for where you store or send it — see the Terms of Service.

None of the above is ever sent to us or to any third party. There is no account system, no analytics, and no tracking in Burn Locker.

3. The “runs on every website” permission

Starting with this version, the browser extension includes a component that runs on the pages you visit, so it can notice when you submit an ordinary login form and offer to save it. Your browser will show this as a broad-sounding permission (“read and change all your data on all websites you visit”) — this is standard for any browser extension that offers this feature (the same permission password managers like 1Password or Bitwarden require), and here is exactly what it does and doesn’t do with it:

  • It watches for ordinary form submissions containing a password field.
  • When one happens, it reads only that form’s hostname, username, and password — nothing else on the page — and hands them to the extension’s own background process on your device.
  • That data is used only to show you the “save this login?” banner and, if you say yes, to encrypt and store the entry in your local vault.
  • None of it is ever sent off your device. There is no server on the other end. It never leaves your browser.
  • If your vault is locked when this happens, the captured login is held in memory only (never written to disk) until you next unlock the extension, at which point it’s offered to you there.

4. Your choices and controls

  • The “save this login?” banner can be turned off permanently for any individual site via “Never for this site,” at any time.
  • You can delete your entire vault at any time via Settings → Delete vault. This is immediate, local, and permanent — see the Terms of Service for details.
  • You can also configure a duress password (Settings → Duress password) that silently wipes the vault if it’s ever used at the unlock screen.

5. Third-party hosting (web app only)

The mobile web app is static — no server-side code of ours runs at all — but it has to be hosted somewhere to be reachable over HTTPS (for example, GitHub Pages). Whoever hosts it may log standard, ordinary web server access data (such as IP address and request timestamps) as part of normal web hosting, the same as any website you visit. This logging is outside our control and is governed by that host’s own privacy practices, not this policy. It has no connection to your vault data, which never leaves your device regardless of how the page itself was served to you.

6. Children’s privacy

Burn Locker is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect information from anyone — which, by design, includes children, since it doesn’t knowingly collect information from anyone at all.

7. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, we’ll update the effective date above. Since the Service doesn’t collect contact information from you, we have no way to notify you directly — check back here if you want to know about changes.
